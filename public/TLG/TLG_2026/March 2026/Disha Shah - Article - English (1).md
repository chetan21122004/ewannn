# **What does "living in more than one language" mean to you** 

To me, living in more than one language is like possessing several different keys to various worlds. Each language holds its own unique significance, shaping how I think, work, and connect with those around me. It isn’t just about translation; it is about the distinct character each tongue brings to my life. 

My journey begins at home with **Gujarati** , my mother tongue. There is an irreplaceable warmth in conversing with my loved ones in Gujarati. It is the language of my roots, my childhood, and my heart. When I speak it, there is a natural ease and intimacy that no other language can replicate. It is the sound of family and comfort. 

However, my creative expression takes a completely different turn with **Hindi** , our national language. I choose to write my blogs exclusively in Hindi because it offers a unique emotional depth. Whenever I switch to Hindi, I feel a shift in my perspective—it is a language that demands a certain soulfulness. Writing in Hindi feels "different" in a profound way; it allows me to tap into a more analytical and poetic side of my personality that stays hidden in daily chores. 

Living in **West Bengal** adds yet another layer to my linguistic identity. Being surrounded by the sweetness of **Bengali** allows me to integrate into my local environment here in Kolkata. Whether I am at a local market or speaking with neighbors, Bengali provides a sense of belonging and cultural immersion. Then, there is **English** , which occupies a completely different space. It is the language of my professional world. Speaking English during a job interview or a formal meeting brings out a sense of precision and global connection that feels entirely separate from my domestic life. 

While I cherish this diversity, I sometimes think about the practical side of things. I often feel that if the national language—Hindi—were made mandatory across every city, it would create a stronger bridge and make life much easier for everyone. 

Ultimately, our country is a beautiful mosaic of identities. Living with multiple languages is an exhilarating experience because it allows me to explore and delve into various cultures simultaneously. To speak more than one language is not just to talk; it is to live multiple lives at once, each with its own identity and definition.
