# Be Brave, You Women By Meera Kathija 

Women, ladies, girls—the names change as she grows, but her struggles remain strikingly similar. Society may evolve in language and appearance, yet the weight placed upon a woman’s life continues to follow a familiar pattern. From childhood to adulthood, she is shaped not by her own choices, but by expectations designed long before she could even understand them. 

Roles are assigned to her, not chosen by her. She is told who to be, how to behave, what to pursue, and what to sacrifice. Behind her silence lies a storm—thousands of psychological battles that remain unseen, unheard, and often unacknowledged. These battles are not always loud; sometimes they exist in quiet doubts, in second-guessing, in the fear of being judged for simply being oneself. 

At every step she takes, there is resistance. Someone pulls her back. Someone labels her—“not enough.” These labels shape perception, limit growth, and plant seeds of doubt. Over time, these words begin to echo in her own mind, turning external criticism into internal conflict. 

A woman’s freedom is often measured and approved by others. Her voice trembles—not because she is weak, but because the world decides how loud she is allowed to be. She learns to adjust, to shrink, to fit into spaces that were never designed for her to grow. 

Modern society often equates a woman’s worth with financial independence. A woman who earns is respected; a woman who does not is overlooked. But her value lies in her existence, not income. Her worth is not a number, not a status, not a title—it is inherent, unchanging, and deeply human. 

Once, women were caged openly—denied choices and silenced without question. Today, the cage is invisible. She studies, works, and rises—yet questions follow her shadow. “How dare she?” “Why is she ahead?” These silent judgments become barriers that she must constantly break. 

The past was an instant poison; the present is a slow one. Restrictions today are subtle, often hidden behind concern, culture, or tradition. This makes them harder to challenge, yet more dangerous because they are rarely acknowledged. 

A quiet woman is called “good.” A dreaming woman is called “difficult.” A bold woman is called “dangerous.” These labels reveal society’s discomfort with women who refuse to fit into predefined roles. It raises a simple yet powerful question: when will a woman simply be called human? 

The world watches her—not to protect, but to judge. Her walk, her clothes, her voice, her 

choices—everything becomes a question. And still, she is told, “Ignore it.” But ignoring does not erase reality. 

Society has countless rules for women: don’t go out too much, don’t stay out too late, don’t dream too big, don’t live too freely. Yet for men, there is often silence. This imbalance reflects a deeper issue—one that continues to shape generations. 

The word “shame” still wears a woman’s face, while respect remains negotiable. This must change. And real change does not begin by controlling women—it begins by raising better men. Teaching respect, kindness, and empathy from a young age can reshape the future. 

There are stories rarely told—stories of women forced into survival, into darkness, into lives they never chose. Instead of compassion, they receive judgment. Society often criticizes what it fails to understand. 

A woman carries pain like hidden fire. She smiles even when breaking. She stands even when collapsing inside. Yet she is called “emotional,” as if emotions are weaknesses rather than strength. 

Marriage, often seen as a promise, can become a sacrifice. She leaves her world behind, yet is expected to fit into another without pause. Rarely does anyone ask what it truly costs her. 

All she asks for is simple: love, respect, space, and understanding. Not control. Not silence. Not judgment. These are not luxuries—they are basic human needs. 

She is not weak. She is not less. She is not defined by expectations. She is strength, resilience, and courage. 

So rise, woman. Rise beyond limits. Rise beyond fear and doubt. Be calm when the world is loud. Be fierce when the world is wrong. 

You are not just surviving—you are shaping the future. Every step you take creates a path for another girl to walk with less fear. 

Be brave. Be unbreakable. Be unstoppable. 

And even when you fall—rise again, stronger than before. Because being a woman is not a limitation. 

It is power. And that power has the ability to change the world.
